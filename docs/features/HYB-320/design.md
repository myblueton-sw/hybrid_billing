# Outcome and ownership

HYB-320, machine:mac_mini, 2026-11-25. This document proposes one new read and the console change that uses it, and asks for independent design review before any product code changes. The read exposes organization structure of a workspace, so the tenant boundary and the public contract are in scope of the review. No command, no amount and no permission rule changes.

Requirements: UI-04 (`docs/planning/v1.16/ui-design.md:547`, filter, page and selection agree; no full load into the browser) and the O01 screen specification (`docs/ui/screens/O01/spec.md:31-33`, a lazily loaded tree in which only permitted nodes are searched and expanded and no private child count is shown; `:102`, lists are read at a fixed revision by cursor and the whole data set is never read into browser memory). HYB-300 left the tree as an open finding (`docs/features/HYB-300/plan.md:129`, `:633`, `:675`).

# Facts read on main 003c101

1. The tree view reads `GET /api/organizations?kind=hierarchy` in pages of 99 and follows up to 10 pages by itself (`organizations/Live.tsx:20-21`, `:153-176`). Every page repeats all parties and all billing profiles of the workspace (`server/organizations.go:194-201`); the server refuses the read when either exceeds 500 (`:188`).
2. The browser builds the tree from those pages (`organizations/live-model.ts:26-73`):
   - a member of the hierarchy relationship with exactly one target covering the day is a child of that target (source "relationship");
   - a member with no covering target is listed under "No organization on the day"; a member with more than one is listed under "conflicts";
   - a party that is not a hierarchy member at all is a child of its registry parent (source "registry"), or a root when it has none;
   - a target that is neither a party nor a member appears as a node of unknown kind, and so does a registry parent that is not a known party (`:50`);
   - nodes that neither a root nor a child of a held node (no organization, conflict, not placed yet) reaches are listed under "cycles", flat and without their children (`:56-67`); the children of a held node are shown under it; parties whose hierarchy page has not been loaded yet are listed under "not placed yet";
   - siblings are ordered by kind (legal entity, internal unit, account, unknown), then name, then identifier.
3. One load follows at most 990 hierarchy members; beyond that the tree is incomplete, says so and offers to load more (`Live.tsx:760-764`). "Expand all" opens every loaded node (`:722`). Every legal-entity root is opened when the tree is first built (`:472-477`).
4. The same read already returns the members of one target on the day, paged by member identifier, through `target=` (`server/orgfilter.go:187-196`, `relationships.MembersOn`, `modules/relationships/registry.go:197`), and the members with no target on the day through `unattributed=true` (`orgfilter.go:58-61`, `:125`).
5. A relationship revision is immutable and is addressed by number; the read accepts `revision=` and refuses a number above the active one (`organizations.go:178-183`). Names are not part of the revision: party and member names come from a fixed map of the sample data (`server/orgseed.go:10`, `:31`), and names of targets also pass through draft labels that can change (`server/groupdrafts.go:75-80`). The party registry is not part of the revision either; it is read as it is now.
6. A legal entity has no registry parent; the registry parent of an internal unit is an active legal entity and cannot change after it is set (`modules/organizations/registry.go:126-147`). The registry alone therefore holds no cycle. The plan validation refuses a cycle inside the hierarchy relationship (`modules/relationships/validate.go:116-134`, called from `plan.go:195`). The one cycle left is across the two sources: a legal entity L with a hierarchy timeline whose chain of targets reaches an internal unit that has no hierarchy timeline and whose registry parent is L. Every cycle therefore contains a legal entity that is a hierarchy member. Not verified: whether any command checks hierarchy targets against party kinds, which would make even that cycle impossible, and whether a party can be deleted.
7. A hierarchy member cannot have two targets covering one day in an activated revision: every kind except the view group is exclusive (`modules/relationships/types.go:41`), overlapping entries of an exclusive kind are refused for every chunk (`validate.go:36`, `plan.go:107`), and a revision is built only from sealed chunks (`plan.go:194`, `:260-262`). The "conflicts" list of the tree can therefore not occur for the hierarchy kind (settled by the design review reading the code).
8. The read is admitted by one decision, installation administration of the caller's workspace (`server/users.go:96-123`). No rule restricts a caller to part of the organization of a workspace, so today every node of the workspace is permitted to every admitted caller.
9. The O02 form reads up to 10 pages of 99 members for each of the five relationship kinds to offer choices (`changes/Live.tsx:42-68`).

# Proposed read

`GET /api/organizations/tree`, admitted exactly like `GET /api/organizations` (same lock, same decision, same `provider` refusal).

| Parameter | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `on` | Day, as in the existing read. Default today (UTC). |
| `revision` | Pinned relationship revision, as in the existing read; a number above the active revision is `invalid_request`. |
| `parent` | Identifier of the node whose children are read. Absent: the roots. |
| `group` | `detached` instead of `parent`: the legal entities that lie on a cycle on the day (R3). `parent` and `group` together are `invalid_request`. |
| `after`, `limit` | Cursor and page size. `limit` 1 to the relationship page size minus one, default 50. |

Answer: `revision`, `relationship_revision`, `active_revision`, `on`, `parent`, `group`, `nodes`, `next`, `scan_stopped`. Each node carries `id`, `name`, `kind` (`legal_entity`, `internal_unit`, `account`, `unknown`), `state` (party state, empty for others), `source` (`relationship`, `registry`, `none`) and `children` (`some`, `none`, `unchecked`). The answer also carries `registry_empty` (the workspace has no party) so that the screen can choose its empty sentence without the party list. It carries no workspace facts; the screen header keeps reading those from the existing read with a page size of one.

Rules, written so that the union of all pages equals the tree of fact 2 for the same revision and day:

- R1 children. The children of P are (a) the hierarchy members whose target covering the day is P, and (b) the parties whose registry parent is P and that have no hierarchy timeline in the revision. This holds for any P, including a member that has no organization on the day, so the rows of that list can be opened like any other node.
- R2 roots. The roots are (a) the parties with no hierarchy timeline and no registry parent, and (b) the identifiers that are the covering hierarchy target of some member on the day, are not parties and have no hierarchy timeline (kind `unknown`). One exception keeps the paging moving: a target of that kind whose former members outnumber the whole budget of a page, none of those examined covering the day, is returned as a root with `children: unchecked`; it may turn out to have nothing under it on the day, and the console rule for a node that turns out empty applies. In that case alone the pages hold a node that the present tree would not show.
- R3 detached. A legal entity is detached when it has a covering hierarchy target on the day and following parents from it (the covering target of a member, the registry parent of a party without a timeline) returns to it. A cycle can pass through several legal entities, each joined to the next by a stretch of hierarchy targets and one registry parent, and only each stretch is limited by the depth limit, so the walk is not cut off per entity. The read walks from every legal entity with a hierarchy timeline, marks each node as unvisited, on the current path or done, and remembers the result; a walk ends at a root, at a member with no covering target, at a node already done, or at a node on the current path, which closes a cycle whose legal entities are then listed. Each node is walked once, so all walks together take at most the party cap times the depth limit plus one steps. Only legal entities with a hierarchy timeline start a walk (fact 6). The group lists the legal entities on a cycle and nothing else; every other node of the cycle, and every node hanging below it, is reached by opening them through R1. This differs from the present "cycles" list, which shows every unreached node flat: the nodes shown are the same once opened, the grouping is not.
- R4 order and cursor. A page is built in two phases. Phase one: the parties that satisfy the rule (at most 500 examined, each by one lookup of its timeline or its registry parent), ordered by kind rank and then identifier. Phase two: the other nodes in identifier order, read from an index the revision already keeps sorted — for children the members of P on the day that are not parties (accounts), for roots the hierarchy targets that are not parties (unknown). The cursor is opaque to the caller: a phase letter, a colon and the last identifier returned; it is checked against the identifier grammar and anything else is `invalid_request`. In phase one the kind rank of the cursor's party is taken from its kind, which cannot change (`modules/organizations/registry.go:147`); if that party no longer exists the cursor is `invalid_request` and the screen reloads the list. The detached group has phase one only. The order no longer uses the name. With today's fixed names an order by name would be computable for the parties; identifier order is chosen so that the rule does not depend on names staying fixed, and it is put to the owner as a choice in the screen review.
- R5 `children`. `some` when R1 gives the node at least one child on the day, `none` when it gives none, `unchecked` when the page's budget (R7) ran out before that was known. No count is returned anywhere in the answer. Under fact 8 the test ranges over every node of the workspace. When a rule that restricts a caller to part of a workspace is introduced, R1, R2, R3 and R5 must be evaluated over the permitted nodes only, a node whose only children are not permitted must answer `none`, and a cursor naming a party the caller may not see must be refused like one that does not exist; this is recorded as a condition on that later work, not built here.
- R6 unknown parent. `parent` naming an identifier that is not a party, not a member and not a target in the revision answers an empty page, not an error, so that the answer does not distinguish "exists elsewhere" from "does not exist". A `parent` that fails the identifier grammar is `invalid_request`. The workspace comes from the session only; no parameter names a workspace.
- R7 bounded work. One page spends at most the configured organization filter scan budget (`org_filter_scan`, default 1000), counted in timeline entries and index positions examined, on all of: phase two candidates, the test of R2(b) for each candidate target, and the `children` test of each returned node. The `children` test and R2(b) use a new module method that stops at the first member covering the day instead of listing all of them. When the budget runs out the page answers what it has, with `scan_stopped: true`, a cursor to continue from and `unchecked` for the nodes not yet tested. Phase one is bounded separately by the party cap: at most 500 lookups, and for `group=detached` at most 500 times the depth limit plus one steps for all walks together. The part of the `children` test that looks for units whose registry parent is the node is computed from the party set and is bounded by the party cap, not by the budget. The read is refused above the party cap like the existing read.
- R8 no relists. The answer does not repeat the parties or the profiles of the workspace.
- R9 registry outside the pin. Because the parties are read as they are now (fact 5), a party created between two pages under the identifier of an account or of an unknown target can make that node appear twice or not at all. The screen keeps one row per identifier within one list of children; a full reload gives the settled picture. When several legal entities lie on one cycle, the same nodes appear under each of their detached rows.

The list "No organization on the day" is not part of the new read; it uses the existing `unattributed=true` filter, and its rows open through R1. No conflicts list is read (fact 7); the console's conflicts section is removed for the tree.

# Console

- The tree view reads the roots, then the children of a node when it is first opened (a node marked `some` or `unchecked` can be opened; one that turns out empty says that it has nothing under it on the day), each list with "Show more" for its next page and its own failure message and retry beside the node. An open node keeps its loaded children while a further page loads.
- "Expand all" is removed: it has no bounded meaning on a lazy tree. "Collapse all" stays.
- "Not placed yet" disappears with its cause. "Cycles" becomes the detached list, read when the tree opens, with the blocked wording of the specification (`spec.md:94`). Its rows can be opened; a node that already appears on the path from the group row to itself is shown once more as a plain row that says so and cannot be opened, which ends every cycle.
- Roots are no longer all opened at the start: that would start one read per legal entity. A single root is opened; otherwise the roots start closed. This is put to the ui-designer review.
- The sentence that the tree shows the loaded structure, and the count of loaded members in the heading, are replaced; the heading shows the revision and the day only.
- The detail of a selected node is read with the existing exact-member filter (`member=`) instead of from the loaded hierarchy pages. A link that names a selected node shows its detail; the tree is not opened down to it (HYB-356).
- The pinned revision, the newer-revision notice and the reset on an invalid pinned revision keep their present behaviour and apply to every tree read.
- A page answered with `scan_stopped` shows the existing "search stopped" wording with "Show more".
- The tree does not gain a search in this ticket. The specification asks that only permitted nodes are searched; the accounts and groups views already search on the server. Locating a node inside the tree needs its chain of ancestors (`relationships.Chain` exists) and is HYB-356.

Copy and composition go to the ui-designer role before the console step is merged; the owner alone accepts the screen.

# O02 choices

Facts read on main b6ec317:

1. When the O02 screen opens, `readChoices` reads up to ten pages of 99 members for each of the five relationship kinds, at the active revision of the first answer, for today (`changes/Live.tsx:42-68`, `:464-477`). It reads again when the active revision changes, after a proposal that the service refused with a member or target field error, and on reload (`:548`, `:640`, `:750`). A proposal that succeeds reads the plans only (`:689`).
2. The loaded members are used for five things: the "Member" select, which lists the loaded members that are not parties and the active internal units (`live-model.ts:21-32`, `Live.tsx:800`); the "Target" select of the pool kinds and the view group, which lists the targets seen in the loaded members (`live-model.ts:34-46`); the present targets of the chosen member on a day (`live-model.ts:48-52`, `Live.tsx:804-813`); whether a member carried by a link is in the list, which decides between the select and the identifier field (`Live.tsx:508-515`); and names for identifiers in the plan lists and the plan detail (`live-model.ts:146-154`, `Live.tsx:127`, `:186-187`, `:202`, `:206-207`, `:934-935`, `:960-961`, `:997-998`).
3. For the hierarchy and payer kinds the "Target" select lists parties only (`live-model.ts:36-37`); the parties come with every answer of the organizations read and are capped at 500.
4. When a kind has more than 990 members, or one of its pages fails (`Live.tsx:54-57`), the lists are incomplete; the screen says so and shows "not in this listing" for a member it did not load (`Live.tsx:842`, `live-model.ts:373-378`).
5. The organizations read finds members by a search term that is matched against the identifier and the name (`server/orgfilter.go:97-100`, `:121`), by exact identifiers (`member=`, at most 50, without `after`, `limit` or `q`; `orgfilter.go:17`, `:71`), and across all kinds for members that are not parties (`subject=account`, `:144-146`, `:197-198`). A search examines at most the scan budget of identifiers from the start of the identifier order and then answers with a cursor and `scan_stopped` (`:197-202`, `:236-239`). The targets read finds targets of one kind by a term matched against the identifier, the name or a draft label (`server/orgtargets.go:51`).
6. O01 already has the pattern needed: a search that sends the term to the server, lists up to ten results and continues from the cursor (`organizations/Live.tsx` history view with `MoreBar`). Its `SearchForm` is a form element of its own (`filters.tsx:41`) and cannot be placed inside the proposal form.

Proposed, with no change to any command and no new read:

- C1 base. One organizations read with a page size of one gives the parties and pins the relationship revision for the other reads of the form, as on O01. It is read again where `readChoices` is read again today.
- C2 member. The select of every member is replaced by a search: a term, a "Search" button and up to ten results as a list of radio choices showing name and identifier. The term is sent as `q` with `subject=account`, so the server results are the members of any kind that are not parties, in identifier order. Active internal units whose identifier or name contains the term are listed before them from the party list when the kind is hierarchy or not yet chosen, as today; they do not count against the ten. Legal entities and inactive parties are not offered, as today. An empty term lists the first ten. The hint says that the search matches names and identifiers. "Enter an identifier instead" stays.
  - Further results: when the answer has a cursor, "Show more" continues from it, for a further page and for a stopped scan alike, so that every member can be reached. A stopped scan that has found nothing yet shows the stopped-search sentence, never "No member matches."
  - The search is not a form inside the proposal form. Enter in the term field runs the search and never sends the proposal; a test pins this.
  - The chosen member is always shown on a line of its own above the search, with name where known, identifier and "Change", including a member carried by a link while its read is pending and a member that is in no result list. That line shows exactly the value the proposal will send. A choice survives a new search. Changing the kind keeps the chosen member, as today.
- C3 target. For hierarchy and payer the select of parties stays. For the pool kinds and the view group it is replaced by the same search against the targets read of that kind, with the same rules for further results, Enter and the line that shows the chosen target. The chosen member is left out of the target results, as today. "Enter an identifier instead" stays.
- C4 present targets. After a member and a kind are chosen, one exact read (`member=`, that kind, the pinned revision, no `subject`) gives the member's whole history, from which the present targets on today and on the chosen day are shown as today; changing the day reads nothing. Its states are loading, failed with "Try again", none of this kind (the answer has no member), none on the day, and the list. "Not in this listing" disappears: the exact read answers for any member. An identifier typed by hand is read only once it is a valid identifier; until then the line shows nothing, as today.
- C5 a member carried by a link. An exact read with `subject=account` decides whether the member exists in the revision as a member that is not a party; an active internal unit counts as known from the party list only when the kind is hierarchy or not set. If the member is neither, or the read fails, the identifier field is shown, as today for a member that is not listed or a listing that failed. Once the person has edited the form the mode is kept, as today.
- C6 names. Plan rows use the member and target names they carry, including in the lead cell of the detail, which today ignores the carried target name. The before and after target lists of the plan detail carry identifiers only; they are named from the party list or from a result the person has seen in this session, otherwise the identifier is shown. Compared with today, pool and view group targets in those two lists can lose their names. No read is made only to name an identifier. This loss is put to the ui-designer review and to the owner.
- C7 states and order. Each search has its own loading line, failure with "Try again" and empty sentence. Every answer is applied only if it still belongs to the present key: term, kind and pinned revision for a search; member, kind and pinned revision for the present targets. When the active revision changes, the base, every result list and the present targets are dropped and read again together. A refused or failed search is never shown as no results. A denied answer switches the whole screen to its denied state and an ended session to signing in, as today. The "loading choices" line and the "incomplete" note are removed with their cause.

Not changed: the commands and their fields, the plan lists, the impact view, the handoff from O01.

Limits: a member that is an account in no relationship of any kind cannot be found by search and must be entered by identifier, as today.

# Limits and open points

- The parties and profiles are still read whole by the other views (cap 500). Removing that is outside this ticket; it belongs with HYB-305.
- R3 is evaluated on each `group=detached` request over at most 500 parties. No measurement exists for a workspace near the cap. If the party cap is lifted (HYB-305), phase one of R4 is no longer bounded and needs an index by kind.
- Phase two of a children page walks the members the target ever had in the revision from the cursor on, filtering by day, under the budget of R7; a target with a long history of former members can need several `scan_stopped` pages before the next child appears.
- Assumption: sibling order by identifier is acceptable to the owner. If the owner requires order by name, the read needs a name snapshot per revision, which does not exist.
- Not verified here: any behaviour on operating systems other than the development machine.
