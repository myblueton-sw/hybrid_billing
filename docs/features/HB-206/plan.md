# Decision

One Go package, `modules/formula`, standard library and the decimal primitive only. It has a pure part, the compilation and the evaluation of a formula set, and an in-memory registry for the path of a revision. Nothing calls it and no entry point is wired. This section is the design after the first design review, whose findings are resolved at the end of this document.

What this can and cannot show: that a formula is a finite tree of nodes of ten closed kinds, a data model in which no code and no call has a form; that a set which compiles has bounded size, consistent types, no reference to an unknown name, no cycle and at most one formula per target and rank; that evaluating a compiled set is a function of its content, the profile, the limits and the input, exact inside, and returns no number, with a reason, where a variable is not known, the divisor of a `div` is zero or the margin of a `gross_up` is outside its range; that a result of the registry names the revision and the digest of the content it came from; that a revision's content never changes; that an approval is recorded only for a subject digest that covers the workspace, the set, the revision, the content and the impact record in force, by another than the author, and stops holding whenever the set's active revision changes; and that a revision is active only with such an approval. It cannot know that a formula is what the customer agreed, that an impact record is true or complete, who an approver is or whether they may approve, or that a result was used. The subject digest binds an approval to what this registry holds; it is not a check of permission or of the network, and SEC01 is not met by it.

Names. A recorded approval is `approval_recorded`, as in the sibling packages. `checked` means compiled without findings under this registry's limits and profile. `impact_recorded` means a caller's statement was stored, a sample included; the registry computes no impact. A revision that is `active` is the one this registry returns for its set; whether a billing uses it is outside. What a formula computes is its target; what an impact counted are subjects.

Not delivered, although the requirement names it: general settings; a text syntax for formulas, a formula being built as a tree by its caller; conditionals, so a price that depends on the whole volume cannot be written, while graduated steps can with `min` and `max`; aggregation scopes, a formula seeing only the variables it is given; the asynchronous computation of a difference over a fixed manifest, of which only the record is kept; code plugins; manual adjustments (AR-07); the approval subject of HB-23 with eligible approvers and limits. The design part of HB-17 stays open with the owner. W-17 asks for independent verification by a DBA and by QA; this ticket has one independent reviewer of design and change, and the rest remains.

Alternatives considered. A parser of expression texts: not taken in this increment; the tree is the contract. Evaluating in the decimal primitive: not taken, for the reason found in HB-204. Letting a failed evaluation fall back to the formula of the next rank: not taken, a missing input would silently change which formula prices a target. Sharing fractions and rounding with `modules/rating`: not possible within this ticket's files; this is the third implementation of the six modes, and moving them into one primitive is recorded as a follow-up.

Every string is an identifier under the project's identifier rule (letters, digits, `. _ : -`, at most 128 bytes), a three-letter uppercase currency code, the text of a number of at most 64 bytes, a digest of 64 lowercase hexadecimal characters, or a value of a closed list. There is no free text. Approver and author identifiers denote people; the caller must make them pseudonymous references (stated obligation). No floating point and no floating or rational type of the standard library is used in the source.

## Types and nodes

A type is one of: `number`, with no currency and no unit; `money` with a currency; `quantity` with a unit, an identifier; `price` with a currency and a unit, money per quantity. A type with a field its kind does not have is malformed. Two types are equal when kind, currency and unit are. Units are compared as written; a product of two units, such as cores times hours, must arrive as one variable of one unit (caller's obligation).

A node has a kind, and by kind: `constant`, a number text and a type; `variable`, a name; `reference`, a formula identifier; the seven operations, exactly two operands. A field its kind does not have must be empty.

- `constant`: its text is one the decimal primitive parses under the profile and may be negative.
- `variable`: its type is the declared one.
- `reference`: its type is the declared output type of the formula named, so no order of formulas affects typing. It names a formula by identifier whatever that formula's rank is.
- `add`, `sub`, `min`, `max`: two operands of equal type, giving that type.
- `mul`: a number and any type, in either order, giving that type; a quantity and a price of the same unit, in either order, giving money of the price's currency; nothing else.
- `div`: any type by a number, giving that type; money by money of one currency, quantity by quantity of one unit, or price by price of one currency and unit, giving a number; money by a quantity, giving a price; money by a price of the same currency, giving a quantity of the price's unit; nothing else.
- `gross_up`: a value of any type and a margin of type number, giving the value divided by one less the margin, of the value's type. It applies to a negative value as to a positive one (−100 at 0.2 gives −125); `modules/rating` instead passes credits at cost, and the two engines differ in this.

The depth of a root is 1.

## Compilation and findings

`Compile(profile, limits, set)` is pure. It returns an invalid error for a profile or limits that are not valid, or else either findings or a compiled set, which is a copy that shares nothing with the caller's set and holds the formulas in an order of evaluation.

A set declares variables, each a name with a type, and formulas. A formula has an identifier, a target, an identifier, a rank from 1 to 999, an output type, a quantum (`1`, `0.1`, `0.01`, `0.001`, `0.0001`), a rounding mode of the six names of HB-204, and a root.

A finding is a code and the identifier of a formula, or no identifier for the set. There is at most one finding per code and formula; findings are returned ordered by the order of codes below and then by identifier in byte order. They repeat nothing else of the content.

Structure is judged first, in a walk that is itself bounded, and stops the compilation when it finds anything:

1. `too_many`, for the set: more variables or more formulas than the limits. It is judged on the two counts before any element is read and is then the only finding.
2. `too_large` and `too_deep`, per formula: a tree is walked counting every visit, so a subtree shared by pointer counts each time it is reached, and the walk of a formula ends at the first visit beyond the limit of nodes, or does not descend below the limit of depth, which also ends a tree that contains itself. Such a formula gets that one finding.
3. `malformed`, for the set or per formula: a string longer than its form allows, judged on its length before its content; an identifier, currency, number, type, kind, quantum or mode outside its form; a quantum the profile cannot read; a rank outside its range; a variable name twice or a formula identifier twice (for the set); a node whose fields do not fit its kind. A walk never descends into operands of a kind that has none.

Only a set without structural findings is judged further, all of the following being reported together:

4. `unknown_name`: a variable or reference the set does not have.
5. `type_mismatch`: an operation whose operand types the rules do not allow, or a root whose type is not the output type. A node with an operand that has no type has no type and gives no further finding.
6. `constant_division_by_zero`: a `div` whose divisor is a constant node of value zero, by value, so `0.00` and `-0` count.
7. `margin_out_of_range`: a margin that is a constant node of value below zero or at least one. Nothing is folded: a divisor or margin that is an operation on constants is judged at evaluation.
8. `cycle`: a formula that refers, directly or through others, to itself, or to a formula that does; found by removing, repeatedly and without recursion, the formulas all of whose references are already removed.
9. `rank_conflict`: each formula that has the target and the rank of another.

## Evaluation

`(*Compiled).Evaluate(target, inputs)` is pure and runs without the registry's lock. It computes the formula of the target with the lowest rank number; formulas of other ranks for that target are computed only where a reference names them (provisional). A target without a formula is not found.

Inputs are a list of a name and a text. More inputs than the limit of variables is a limit error, judged before any is read. A name the set does not declare, a name twice, and a text that is neither empty nor a number are malformed. The empty text and an absent variable mean not known. A value may be negative for every type.

Values are exact fractions of whole numbers, kept with a denominator above zero and in lowest terms after every operation. After every operation the numerator and the denominator are measured in decimal digits, and one beyond the limit of digits is a limit error for the call.

The formulas the computed one reaches through references are computed first, each once, in the compiled order (a formula after all it refers to; among those ready, by identifier in byte order), so recursion never goes deeper than one tree. A limit error in any of them is the call's error. Within a tree the left operand is evaluated wholly, then the right, then the node's own test, and the first reason met ends the formula: `variable_not_known`; `division_by_zero`, a `div` whose divisor has the value zero; `margin_out_of_range`, a margin below zero or at least one, so a margin of exactly one has this reason. A reference to a formula without a value has that formula's reason. No reason is ever turned into zero.

Rounding. A formula's exact value is rounded once to its quantum under its mode, and that is the value returned. A reference to a formula of type money sees the rounded value, since an amount exists only at its quantum; a reference to a formula of another type sees the exact value, so a share or a small unit price loses nothing inside (provisional; the label `formula_v1` names these semantics, is part of the digest and is repeated in every result, so that a later version can tell these semantics from its own). A rounded value the profile cannot hold is a limit error.

A result holds: the label; the profile's identifier and revision; the target; the formula computed; its type; either its value as the primitive prints it and its exact fraction, or its reason; and the formulas it reached, transitively, by identifier in byte order, each with type and value and exact fraction, or reason. Errors of a call: invalid, not found, limit. A call that ends in a limit error has no result; a caller computing an impact counts such subjects as failed.

## Revisions

The registry keeps, per workspace and set identifier, revisions under numbers from 1 to 999,999 that the caller chooses; they may become active in any order. A set has an epoch, a number that starts at zero and rises by one at every activation and at every withdrawal of the active revision, and a sequence number that every changing write raises.

`Draft(workspace, set, revision, author, content)` runs the structural part of the compilation first and refuses content with `too_many`, `too_large` or `too_deep` as a limit error and with `malformed` as invalid; so content is bounded before it is copied, printed or hashed, and a stored draft has unique names and well-formed nodes. It then stores a copy that shares nothing with the caller's, the author and the content digest. The content of a revision never changes: a repeat with identical content and author writes nothing, anything else under the same number is a conflict. `Content` returns a copy.

The content digest is the SHA-256 of a print in which every field is written as its length in eight bytes, big-endian, followed by its bytes, and every number as its decimal text in such a field: the label `formula_v1`; the count of variables, then each variable in byte order of name as name, kind, currency, unit; the count of formulas, then each formula in byte order of identifier as identifier, target, rank, kind, currency, unit, quantum, mode and its root; a node as its kind followed by, for a constant, its text, kind, currency and unit, for a variable its name, for a reference its identifier, and for an operation its two operands. Absent currency and unit are empty fields. Number texts are hashed as written, so `1` and `1.0` are different contents.

States: `draft`, `check_failed`, `checked`, `impact_recorded`, `approval_recorded`, `active`, `superseded`, `withdrawn`.

- `RunCheck` compiles a draft, under the lock, a cost bounded by the limits and accepted. Without findings the state is `checked` and the compiled set is kept; with findings it is `check_failed`, the findings are kept, and the revision can only be withdrawn. On a revision in a later state other than withdrawn it returns the state and findings without writing.
- `RecordImpact(…, impact)` takes a manifest identifier and its digest, the epoch the impact was computed in, coverage `full` or `sample`, and the numbers of subjects evaluated, changed, without a value and failed. Malformed when changed is above evaluated or without a value and failed together are above evaluated. Rejected unless the state is `checked`, `impact_recorded` or `approval_recorded` and the epoch named is the set's epoch now. An identical repeat of the impact in force writes nothing. Otherwise: when an approval is recorded on the impact in force and that impact's epoch is still the set's, a different impact is a conflict; in every other case the new impact replaces the one in force, the earlier one and any approval on it move to the revision's history, which is kept, readable and bounded by a limit, and the state is `impact_recorded`. So a sample, or an impact with subjects without a value, can be followed by a full one.
- The subject digest of a revision with an impact in force is the SHA-256, in the same field form, of: the label `formula_approval_subject_v1`, workspace, set, revision number, content digest, and the impact's manifest identifier, manifest digest, epoch, coverage and four numbers. `Revision` returns it.
- `RecordApproval(…, approver, reference, subject digest)` is rejected unless the state is `impact_recorded`, the impact's epoch is the set's epoch now, its coverage is `full`, no subject is without a value or failed, the digest given equals the subject digest, and the approver is not the author. A full impact over zero subjects is accepted, for a set nobody uses yet (stated). An identical repeat writes nothing; another approval while one is recorded is a conflict.
- `Activate` is rejected unless the state is `approval_recorded` and the impact's epoch is the set's epoch now. The revision becomes `active`, the one that was active `superseded`, and the epoch rises. On the active revision it returns the state.
- Because the epoch rises on every change of the active revision, withdrawal included, every impact and approval recorded before it stops holding and never holds again; `Revision` says whether the impact in force is of the current epoch. This is the invalidation of an approval by a change of policy, at the level of this package.
- `Withdraw(…, reason)` with a reason of `mistake`, `replaced`, `refused` or `stopped` takes out any revision that is not withdrawn; an identical repeat writes nothing and another reason is a conflict. Withdrawing the active revision leaves the set without one and is a change of policy that needs no impact and no approval: a stop switch, not conservative, stated.

`EvaluateRevision(workspace, set, revision, target, inputs)` evaluates any revision that has a compiled set, a withdrawn or superseded one included, so what a revision once gave can be reproduced; it takes the compiled set under the lock, evaluates outside it, and adds set, revision and content digest to the result. `Active` returns the active revision's number and content digest, or zero and the empty text, with epoch and sequence. `Revision` returns state, author, content digest, findings, the impact in force with its subject digest and whether it is current, the approval, the history, the withdrawal and the times of each step. `Revisions(workspace, set, after, limit)` lists numbers and states in ascending order after a number, at most the page size.

## Repeats, error kinds, tenant boundary, time and limits

A request is judged in this order: malformed by what the request alone shows, invalid, with sizes before contents; what it names looked up, an unknown set or revision, or one of another workspace, being not found (`Draft` creates the set); a repeat when identical, a conflict as named above; what a stored state or rule refuses, rejected; no room, a limit error. Errors are fixed values and repeat no string of the request.

Every operation takes the workspace and reaches only that workspace's sets. The registry authorizes nothing and must not be exposed directly (exposure blocker). It is built with limits, a valid decimal profile and a clock; its time is the highest reading of the clock, in UTC. Limits, each required, greater than zero and with an upper bound: variables and formulas per set, nodes (at most 10,000) and depth (at most 100) per formula, digits of a whole number in an evaluation, sets per workspace, revisions per set, impacts kept per revision, and the page size. One lock guards the registry.

## What is not decided here

Provisional defaults, each to be confirmed or changed by the owner: the ten kinds of node and four types; one rounding per formula; a reference sees a rounded amount and an exact value of any other type; the lowest rank number applies and nothing falls back; an approver other than the author is the only separation of duties checked; an approval needs a full impact with no subject without a value or failed. Not conservative, stated: an impact record is a caller's statement and only its contradictions are refused; nothing verifies a manifest's digest; an author and an approver are identifiers that nothing ties to a person; the active revision can be withdrawn without approval. Costs to availability: a revision whose check fails cannot be repaired, only replaced; failed and withdrawn revisions use up a set's numbers and room for good; every change of the active revision sends pending revisions back to their impact; a compilation runs under the registry's lock. Assumptions: a formula's inputs are scalars given by the caller; units are identifiers compared as written.

# Design re-review resolution

The independent re-review of 2026-11-13 returned PASS with findings and no blocker: F1 to F21 closed or partly closed, new findings N1 to N12. The rules below are part of the design and prevail over the sections above where they differ.

- N1, the margin: the test of a margin uses the exact value. A margin formula of exact value 0.99996 and quantum 0.0001 is shown as `1.0000` and a `gross_up` by a reference to it computes; one of exact value 1.00004 is refused with `margin_out_of_range`. A margin written as a division by one less a rate is a `div` and is not a margin; only the margin of a `gross_up` is judged.
- N2, values that are not money: for a formula of type number, quantity or price the exact fraction is the result and the rounded value is its display at the quantum. For a formula of type money the rounded value is the result. The entry of a reached formula of type money shows the rounded value the reference used.
- N3, reached: the formulas a formula reaches are every formula named by a reference node anywhere in its tree, and theirs in turn, whether or not an evaluation would arrive at the node. All are computed and rounded in the call, so a limit error in any of them, an unholdable rounded value included, comes before any reason.
- N4, order of error kinds: the sizes of a request come first and are a limit error (counts of variables, formulas and inputs, then the size and depth of every tree); then the form of the request, invalid; then the lookup; then repeats and conflicts; then rejections; then room. For `Draft`, a set with one formula too large and another malformed is a limit error. An identical repeat of the impact in force or of its approval writes nothing and succeeds also when its epoch is past; `Revision` shows that it is not current.
- N5, the structural walk: nodes are values, so no operand is absent. A walk visits a node, counts it, and ends the formula with `too_large` when the count is beyond the limit, else with `too_deep` when the node's depth is beyond the limit; it follows operands only of the seven operation kinds and only when there are exactly two, left first. An operation with another number of operands, and any other kind with operands, is `malformed`. After `too_many`, set-level `malformed` is judged: a variable's name or type outside its form, a formula's identifier outside its form, a name or an identifier twice; a formula whose identifier is outside its form gets no finding of its own, since it cannot be named. Then `too_large`, `too_deep` and `malformed` are reported together for all formulas, a formula with one of the first two not being judged for the third.
- N6: `Draft` makes its copy in the counted walk and judges the copy; duplicates are looked for after lengths.
- N7: the history holds the impacts that were replaced, each with its approval if any, not the one in force. When it is full `RecordImpact` is a limit error and nothing is written; such a revision can only be withdrawn and drafted again under another number (cost to availability).
- N8: references to formulas the set does not have are disregarded in the judgment of cycles. The removal is a worklist over counts of unresolved references, linear in formulas and references.
- N9, upper bounds of limits: variables 1,000; formulas 1,000; nodes 10,000; depth 100; digits 10,000; sets per workspace 100,000; revisions per set 999,999; impacts in a history 1,000; page size 1,000. A compilation costs at most formulas times nodes visits and an evaluation as many operations on whole numbers of at most twice the digits; this product at the bounds is accepted under the lock for compilation. The number of workspaces is the caller's bound.
- N10, costs and gaps, stated: a recorded approval of the current epoch cannot be taken back and its impact cannot be corrected except by withdrawing the revision; a superseded revision does not become active again, a return being a new number with the same content; `RecordImpact`, `Activate` and `Withdraw` record no actor. An approval record keeps the subject digest it was given.
- N11: profile and limits are not part of the subject digest. A value does not depend on them, only whether there is an error; with persistence a change of profile would be a change the epoch does not see. Not decided here.
- N12: the first operand of `gross_up` is the value and the second the margin. A number text the profile cannot read, for its syntax or its bounds, is malformed, in a constant and in an input. `EvaluateRevision` on a revision without a compiled set, and `RunCheck` on a withdrawn revision, are rejected.
