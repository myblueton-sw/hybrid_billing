It is the nineteenth increment built under the owner's instruction of 2026-11-12. Base: main `3163a13`. Root (Claude Code) owns planning, implementation, tests and integration. A separate read-only reviewer reviews this plan before implementation and the change afterwards, because the model produces the quantities a period is charged by and holds history per tenant. Machine: mac_mini.

# Observed baseline

Facts read on 2026-11-13:

- Requirement HB-13: a charge for a period is computed from the history of configuration and metering. The present size of a VM, or a CPU utilisation, is not turned into the charge of a whole past month. Proposed direction: a stable asset identifier with the validity periods of creation, deletion, size and organisation; counters with kind, unit, collection interval, reset or replacement and gaps; an approved local daily aggregate where the source does not keep history long enough. Scale: split the source's query limit and time window and keep checkpoints; join intervals by an index of asset and period and a sorted merge.
- Acceptance of HB-13: a change of size within a month, a gap, a counter reset, DST, GB against GiB and kW against kWh are verified; missing history is neither zero nor a normal completion. Linked source cases DC01 (past VM changes, gaps and meter resets: exact attribution per period, a missing measurement is not zero), PO06 (VMware usage and unit price kept apart) and SIM06 (units of usage, assignment and power, and gaps) are NOT_RUN.
- Open decisions of HB-13, not made: the vCenter version, the retention of counters, the billing unit, the contract of allocated against used. No vCenter interface, counter name or retention figure is in the planning files read.
- Existing code: `modules/hawkeye`, `modules/tokenmeter` and `modules/costpool` have the patterns reused here: facts written once under the caller's identifier, repeats and conflicts by compared texts, a void as the exit, fixed error values, a sequence number of the source, pages. `modules/connectors` and `modules/intake` are where query windows and checkpoints belong.

# Decision

One Go package, `modules/vmhistory`, standard library only, with an in-memory registry. It calls no vCenter and has no entry point wired in this increment: an adapter that read history reports assets, configuration spans, counters and readings, and the registry answers what a period contains and what it lacks.

What this can and cannot show: that the quantities of a period are the exact integrals of the reported configuration over the time it was reported for; that a size is converted from the unit it was stated in; that energy is summed only between two readings of one counter that are next to each other, of the same generation, in order, close enough and wholly inside the period, and never across a reset, a decrease, a gap or a period boundary; and that every second of an asset's life in the period that no span or no such pair of readings covers is counted as uncovered and makes the reading `incomplete`. It cannot know that a report is what vCenter said, that a span is the configuration that was really in force and was not filled backward from a present state, that the stated unit is the unit the source meant (a source may label binary quantities `MB`), that a counter measures the asset it is reported for, that a meter that kept counting lost nothing, or that history that was never reported existed. `complete` says that the reported history covers the window, not that it is true.

The requirement that a present size is not turned into a past month is met here only in part and by obligation: a span has a start and an end, an end may not lie after the registry's time, and there is no input for a size without a time, a utilisation, a rate or a price; but an adapter that knows only today's size can report one span from creation to now and nothing notices. A span carries no basis of observation; whether it should is a question for the owner. A configuration span is what was allocated. Whether a customer is charged for what was allocated or what was used is not decided, and used CPU or memory is not received at all (PO06, usage and unit price apart, only in that no price exists here).

Not delivered, although the requirement names it: counter kinds other than a cumulative energy counter, in particular a counter that reports an amount per interval and a unit of joules, and a collection interval as such (a counter states the longest distance that still pairs two readings); a local daily aggregate where the source's retention is short; query windows and checkpoints, which belong to the connector; power as a rate integrated over time; parallel meters of one asset; the attribution of a host's energy to its VMs or of energy to an organisation; a reference from a fact to the intake artifact it came from (HB-06). The shape of a counter is provisional until the vCenter decision. The design part of HB-13 stays open with the owner. Of DC01 this addresses, at the level of this package, attribution of configuration per period and that a missing measurement is not zero; of SIM06 units and gaps; nothing of either at system level. W-13 asks for independent verification by QA and a domain reviewer; this ticket has one independent reviewer of design and change, and the rest remains.

Alternatives considered. A current configuration per asset with a log of changes, from which spans are derived: not taken, a derived span rests on the assumption that nothing changed between two events the adapter saw. Interpolating a counter to a period boundary: not taken, it invents a value. A reset as a mark on a reading: not taken, the mark is lost when that reading is voided or arrives late; a generation number on every reading survives both.

Every caller-supplied string is an identifier under the project's identifier rule (letters, digits, `. _ : -`, at most 128 bytes), of which the package has its own copy, a whole number, the text of a counter value, or a value of a closed list. There is no free text. Host names, addresses and display names fit the identifier rule and often carry customer or personal names: the adapter must report the source's opaque identifier for an asset and a pseudonymous reference for an organisation (stated obligation). An organisation is resolved nowhere. No floating point is used; all arithmetic is exact on integers of any size. All times are instants, kept and returned in UTC, in whole seconds, an instant with a fraction being malformed; intervals are half-open, the start included and the end not. A month of a zone with daylight saving time is the two instants the caller gives; the package does no arithmetic of zones, and whoever computes the instants carries that risk.

## Forms

A whole number has the form `0|[1-9][0-9]*`. A revision and a generation are numbers from 1 to 999,999. A counter value has the form of a whole number of at most fifteen digits with an optional point and one to six digits after it; it is never negative and has no exponent. Texts are kept as written and compared as texts in a repeat, so `1.50` against `1.5`, and `1 GiB` against `1024 MiB`, are different reports. A fact that can be voided is `live` or `voided`; every write returns that state, so a repeat of a voided fact succeeds and says `voided`.

## Sources and assets

Everything is kept per workspace and source, a source being one vCenter as the adapter names it. One installation reported under two source identifiers escapes every rule between its facts (stated obligation). Workspaces are not limited in number (stated).

`PutAsset(workspace, source, asset)` reports an asset with an identifier, a kind (`vm`, `host`), the instant it was created, the instant it was deleted or none, and a revision. The kind is a label that no rule uses. The first report creates the asset. A report with the stored revision is a repeat when identical and a conflict otherwise; a lower revision is rejected as stale, an old retry included; a higher one replaces kind and lifetime, and is rejected unless every live span and every live reading of a live counter of the asset still lies within the new lifetime. The deletion lies after the creation; neither lies after the registry's time. An asset has no void: one reported by mistake is ended by a revision that deletes it one second after its creation, after its spans and readings were voided; it stays listed. A deletion learned late is reported in this order: void what lies after it, then the revision.

## Configuration spans

`PutSpan(workspace, source, span)` reports that an asset had one configuration from one instant to a later one: an identifier unique in the source, the asset, start and end, a number of virtual CPUs (a whole number up to 1,000,000), a memory size, a disk size, and an organisation that may be none. Each of the three may be not stated (the empty text), so an adapter that knows the history of one and not of another need not invent a value; zero is a value. A size is a whole number of at most 18 digits with a unit of a closed list: `B`, `kB`, `MB`, `GB`, `TB` (powers of 1000) and `KiB`, `MiB`, `GiB`, `TiB` (powers of 1024). It is converted to bytes exactly and kept as written. Any other unit, `Gb`, `gb`, `KB` or one with a space among them, is malformed, so a caller cannot leave the unit to a default.

A span is written once under its identifier: an identical repeat writes nothing and succeeds, a differing one is a conflict. A new one is rejected unless the asset exists, the span lies within the asset's lifetime, its end is not after the registry's time, and it overlaps no other live span of the asset. Spans that touch do not overlap. `VoidSpan(workspace, source, span, reason)` takes a span out with a reason of a closed list (`withdrawn_at_source`, `wrong_report`, `operator`); its identifier stays taken, and the time it covered is free for another span.

## Counters and readings

`PutCounter(workspace, source, counter)` reports a cumulative energy counter: an identifier unique in the source, the asset it measures, a unit of the closed list `Wh`, `kWh`, `MWh`, and the longest distance in seconds between two readings that still pairs them, at least 1 and at most the configured maximum. It is written once. A unit of power, `W` or `kW`, is malformed: a rate is not a counter value. `VoidCounter(workspace, source, counter, reason)` takes a counter with all its readings out of every result; its identifier stays taken. A counter reported for the wrong asset is voided and reported again under another identifier.

`PutReadings(workspace, source, counter, readings)` reports a batch of readings: each an instant, a value, a generation and a revision. The generation says which run of the meter a value belongs to: the adapter raises it whenever the counter was reset or replaced, and two values are comparable only within one generation. A value is kept as written and converted exactly to millionths of a watt-hour. A reading's identity is its counter and instant. A report with the stored revision is a repeat when identical and a conflict otherwise; a lower revision is rejected as stale; a higher one replaces value and generation and makes a voided reading live again, which is the exit for a wrong value. A batch must not be empty or name an instant twice (malformed), and is applied whole or not at all. A reading that is new or replaces is rejected unless its counter is live, its instant lies in the closed interval from the asset's creation to its deletion and not after the registry's time, and its generation is not lower than that of any earlier live reading of the counter and not higher than that of any later one, the other readings of the batch included. `VoidReading(workspace, source, counter, instant, reason)` takes a reading out.

Between two live readings of a live counter that are next to each other in time lies a segment, which is, in this order of tests: `reset`, when their generations differ; `decrease`, when the later value is smaller; `gap`, when they are farther apart than the counter allows; and otherwise `paired`, with the difference of the two values as its energy. Only a paired segment carries energy. `paired` says that two comparable readings stand next to each other, not that the meter counted throughout: a meter that stood still gives paired segments of zero, and a complete result of zero (not conservative, stated). What was consumed across a reset is not known and not estimated; a decrease is not read as a reset; over a gap the difference is not used (conservative, costs availability). Voiding a reading makes its neighbours next to each other, and their generations still tell a reset.

## Reading a period

`Usage(workspace, source, asset, start, end)` reads one asset over a period of whole seconds that runs forward and is not longer than the configured maximum. The period may reach beyond the registry's time. The window is the part of the period in which the asset was alive, deleted or not. It returns the window's length and two results, each `complete`, `incomplete` or `not_applicable`, the last exactly when the window is empty.

Configuration. Covered seconds are those of the window that lie in a live span; uncovered are the rest; the two add up to the window. For every organisation with a span in the window, and apart for none, it returns the seconds and, for each of CPU, memory and disk, the integral over the spans that state it (CPU-seconds, byte-seconds) and the seconds of spans that do not state it. An entry exists exactly when a span contributes. Reasons, in this order: `not_covered`, `cpu_not_stated`, `memory_not_stated`, `disk_not_stated`. It is `complete` exactly when no reason applies. The integrals of an incomplete result are sums over what was stated and nothing else; no part of the window is filled from a neighbouring span. A change of size within a month is two spans, each entering the integral with its own seconds; the spans themselves are read with `Spans`.

Energy. A segment counts for the window when it shares time of positive length with it. Covered seconds are those of the window that lie in at least one paired segment wholly inside the window; uncovered are the rest; the two add up to the window. Per live counter of the asset it returns the sum of its paired segments wholly inside the window and their seconds. The asset's total is the sum over counters, and is absent when no paired segment contributes or when counters overlap. Reasons, in this order: `no_counter` (the asset has no live counter; alone); `counters_overlap` (paired segments of two counters, both counting for the window, share time of positive length; touching at an instant is not sharing); `counter_reset`, `counter_decrease`, `counter_gap` (a segment of that kind counts for the window); `boundary_not_read` (a paired segment counts for the window and is not wholly inside it; its energy is not divided and not counted); `not_read` (some second of the window lies in no segment of any counter). It is `complete` exactly when no reason applies. Then nothing is uncovered, since every second of the window lies in a segment, none of them is of another kind or crosses an edge, and so all are paired and inside; and no second is counted twice. The reverse does not hold: a second counter with a reset beside one that covers the window makes the result incomplete with nothing uncovered. Two meters of one asset at the same time cannot be told from one meter reported twice, so parallel meters are not supported and a total is withheld (stated). For a VM that has no meter by design the energy result is `incomplete` with `no_counter`; a consumer must not read that as a defect of the VM.

Both results come with a sequence number of the source, which every write that changes something raises by one, and a change number of the asset, which is the source's number at the last change of the asset or of any fact of it, so a consumer can tell that what it read is still what is stored without the whole source standing still. `Assets` returns the assets of a source in pages in the order of identifiers after a given one, `Spans` the live spans of an asset in the order of their start, `Counters` the counters of an asset in the order of identifiers with their state, `Readings` the live readings of a counter in the order of time after a given instant. Voided spans and readings are not listed. Nothing is computed for a whole source at once.

## Repeats, error kinds, tenant boundary, time and limits

A request is judged in this order: malformed by what the request alone shows, which is invalid, an empty batch and an instant twice in a batch among it; what it names looked up, an unknown source, asset, span, counter or reading, or one of another workspace, being not found (for a first `PutAsset` of a source, creating it is the normal case); a batch above the batch limit, a limit error; an identifier that exists, a repeat when identical and a conflict otherwise, with a second void under another reason a conflict; what a stored state or rule refuses, rejected, a stale revision among it; no room, a limit error. In a batch a conflict of any reading comes before a rejection of any other, and a batch of repeats only succeeds whatever the state. A repeat and a refusal change nothing and do not raise a number. Errors are five fixed values and repeat no string of the request.

Every operation takes the workspace and reaches only that workspace's sources. The registry authorizes nothing and must not be exposed directly (exposure blocker). It is built with limits and a clock, all required; the clock is read once per operation, reads included, and the registry's time is the highest reading, so a clock that goes back changes nothing. The times of facts come from the source's clock and the registry's from the local one: a reading that is ahead by a second of skew is rejected with its whole batch (cost to availability, stated). Limits, each greater than zero: sources per workspace, assets per source, spans per asset, counters per asset, readings per counter, readings per batch, the page size, the longest period, and the longest distance a counter may allow. Voided facts count towards the limits for good, so a counter that reaches its limit of readings needs a new identifier. One lock guards the registry and every workspace waits on it. Live spans of an asset and live readings of a counter are kept in order of time, voided ones outside that order; a read of a period finds its first span and reading by binary search and scans what counts for the window; a revision of a lifetime looks at the first and last live span and reading only. A period of the longest length with readings a second apart is the stated bound of one read.

## What is not decided here

Not conservative, stated: a span is believed for all of its time; two sources may report the same machine; an organisation is not checked against anything; a meter that stood still reads as zero energy. Costs to availability: energy is complete only when readings lie exactly on both edges of the window, so an adapter must read at period boundaries and at creation and deletion, or the owner must decide how a boundary is settled; every reset, every replacement with time between the meters and every late boundary makes its period incomplete with no rule to settle it; a gap discards a difference that may be good; one shared second of two counters withholds the total. Assumptions: energy counters are cumulative and never negative; one configuration per asset at a time; sizes are whole numbers of their unit. Undecided and not chosen here: the vCenter version and its counters, their kind, unit and retention, the local daily aggregate and who approves it, the billing unit, allocated against used, a basis of observation for a span, how a boundary reading is obtained.

# Design re-review resolution

The narrow re-review of 2026-11-13 (read-only, nothing executed) returned PASS with findings, no blocker and no redesign: of the first findings it held seventeen resolved and five partly; twenty new findings, eleven MEDIUM and nine LOW, none of which changes the model. It confirmed by reading that generations stay ordered under every legal write, that a complete energy result implies nothing uncovered and no second counted twice, and that configuration is additive over adjoining periods. It said implementation may start after text fixes and that a read of the changed sentences is enough. The rules below settle them and are part of the decision; where they are more precise than a sentence above, they govern. The change review reads them against the code.

- N1. The order of generations is checked on the live readings of the counter as they would stand after the whole batch; readings that stay voided take no part.
- N2. An unknown asset is not found, for `PutSpan` and for `PutCounter`, also when the span or counter identifier exists; the sentence that lists an existing asset among the conditions of a rejection is corrected by this.
- N3, what is malformed although it reads like a rule: a deletion that is not after the creation, a span whose end is not after its start, a period that does not run forward or is longer than the maximum, a longest distance of a counter outside its range. The order of generations is a rule of the stored state and rejected, also between two readings of one batch.
- N4. `VoidReading(workspace, source, counter, instant, revision, reason)` names the revision it voids: a revision that is not the stored one is rejected, so a late retry cannot void a corrected reading. A reading made live again by a higher revision has no void reason; voiding it anew may give any reason. The same call on a voided reading of that revision is a repeat with the same reason and a conflict with another.
- N5, the form of results. Seconds are numbers. An integral and an energy are texts: an integral a whole number, an energy a plain decimal in watt-hours without zeros at the end of the fraction. Absent is the empty text. A counter's energy is absent when no paired segment of it contributes; a dimension's integral is absent when no span of the entry states it; the total is absent as said above. So zero is always a stated zero.
- N6. The rules for `complete` and for reasons hold for a window that is not empty. `not_applicable` carries no reasons, no entries, no counters and no total.
- N7, forms of input. A size is a value and a unit; not stated is both empty, and one without the other is malformed. An organisation that is none is the empty text; a deletion that is none is the zero instant; the number of CPUs not stated is the empty text.
- N8. Voiding a counter voids none of its readings, but they are in no result; `Readings` and `VoidReading` on a voided counter are rejected; a batch of repeats for it returns each reading's own state, and a new or replacing reading is rejected.
- N9, work under the lock. A revision of a lifetime looks at the first and last live span of the asset and the first and last live reading of each of its live counters. One read of a period is bounded by the spans of the asset in the window and, for each of its counters, the readings in the window; the overlap of counters is found by one merge of their paired segments.
- N10. A revision of a lifetime judges spans and readings by the rules they were written under: spans in the half-open lifetime, readings in the closed one.
- N11, numbers. A write that changes something raises the source's number by one, a batch by one. The change number of an asset is set to it by a revision of the asset, a span written or voided, a counter written or voided, a batch that writes a reading of one of its counters, and a reading voided. Every page and every result returns the source's number; `Usage`, `Spans`, `Counters` and `Readings` also the asset's.
- N12, pages. `Assets` and `Counters` return what comes after a given identifier, `Spans` what starts after a given instant, `Readings` what lies after a given instant; the empty identifier and the zero instant ask for the first page; the position is exclusive; a page says whether more follows.
- N13. The property about a union is stated per counter: a counter's energy over a union is never less than the sum over the parts, and equal when no paired segment of it crosses the inner boundary. The total is not additive, since it can be withheld.
- N14, exits that cost work: a reset learned late between two stored generations needs every later reading replaced by revision, the latest first, unless the adapter leaves room between generation numbers; a meter replaced at one instant cannot have its last old and first new value in one counter, and is reported as a second counter that starts at that instant.
- N15. Voided facts count towards the limits and an asset cannot be reported under a new identifier, so a long-lived asset can reach a state in which no further history can be reported for it (a capacity limit of this increment).
- N16. Entries of organisations and of counters are returned in the order of identifiers; the entry for no organisation is its own field.
- N17. The total is absent whenever `counters_overlap` applies, also when the overlap involves a segment that crosses an edge and contributes nothing (intended, conservative).
- N18. The answer of `Usage` is called a result, a reading being a meter's.
- N19. The second oracle takes the window closed at its end, so the value on the end instant is the last.
- N20. A first `PutAsset` that is refused creates no source. Voided spans and readings and the reasons of voids are read nowhere but in the answer of a repeated write (accepted for this increment).
