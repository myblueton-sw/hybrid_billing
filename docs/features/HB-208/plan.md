# Decision

Two Go packages, standard library and the decimal primitive only. `packages/primitives/exact` holds exact fractions of whole numbers with a positive denominator in lowest terms; it is the copy of `modules/formula`'s fractions made public, and `modules/rating` and `modules/formula` are not changed to use it in this ticket: until a follow-up ticket, named in the verification record, moves them, four implementations of the six modes exist, each compared with the decimal primitive by its tests. `modules/fx` is an in-memory registry with a clock and a pure conversion on a stored snapshot. Nothing calls either and no entry point is wired. This section is the design after the first design review, whose findings are resolved at the end of this document.

What this can and cannot show: that a rate used in a conversion is one of a stored snapshot, fixed before any line was converted, with the observations, the policy version, the version that allowed a default, the reason for the selection, the construction, the dates and any fallback, the average, the spread and each rounding recorded; that a rounded sales rate is refused when it is further from the exact one than its policy allows; that the same snapshot converts the same line to the same amounts; that a day without a rate gives an earlier recorded rate only where the selected policy allows it, within its bound of elapsed days and on one date for every leg, and otherwise no rate with a reason; that an observation labelled `manual` or `file`, a void and a fixed rate are not used before an approval by another identifier is recorded, the fixed rate also needing evidence; that a line stated to be in the target currency is returned unchanged and labelled. It cannot know that an observation is what the source published, that its origin label is true (a manual value labelled `feed` needs nothing), that a quoted value does not already contain an addition, who a recorder or an approver is, that a policy is what the contract says, that a need's date, customer and contract are the right ones for a line, that a line was not converted in another call or is not itself a converted amount, or that a result was used. It fetches nothing.

Names. An approval is `approval_recorded`; nothing is checked about the approver but that it is not the actor who recorded what it approves. A snapshot is `prepared`, which says its rates were fixed by this registry at a time, not that they are right. An entry without a rate is `held`. An earlier rate is `previous_recorded`, since the registry knows only what was recorded. The order of conversion and charges is the policy's instruction `convert_after_source_charges`, which the package records and cannot enforce.

Not delivered, although the requirement names it: fetching rates, and recording an interface failure as distinct from no rate recorded; a calendar of business days, an earlier recorded day standing for the previous business day; approval of policy versions other than fixed rates, approval of snapshots, and re-approval of an invoice after a rate changed, which belong to HB-23 and W-23, this package giving a snapshot whose rates changed another content digest so that an approval bound to the old one does not cover it; the rate for corrections, an open decision; the rounding difference between lines and a total (COM08); the ERP posting; rates per line item; a spread as an absolute addition; a cross built from a product of legs or from a leg that must be inverted; a quantum tied to its currency. The purposes `display` and `accounting` are labels whose use is not built. The design part of HB-18 stays open with the owner. W-18 asks for independent verification by a DBA and by QA; this ticket has one independent reviewer of design and change, and the rest remains.

Alternatives considered. Looking a rate up when a line is converted: not taken, the requirement forbids it and a run would not be reproducible. Falling back to the MSP default whenever a contract says nothing: not taken, AR-06 asks that the contract allow it. Using the latest observation whenever the wanted one is missing, or the revision below a void one: forbidden by AR-06. Averaging over a month still in progress: not taken. Rounding a rate in the orientation in which it converts: not taken, the inverse of a strong currency loses most of its digits; the rate is rounded in its quoted orientation and unit and checked against the exact one.

Every string is an identifier under the project's rule (letters, digits, `. _ : -`, at most 128 bytes), a three-letter uppercase currency code, a date `YYYY-MM-DD` or a month `YYYY-MM` of the proleptic Gregorian calendar from 2000-01-01 to 2099-12-31, the text of a number of at most 64 bytes, a digest of 64 lowercase hexadecimal characters, or a value of a closed list. There is no free text. Actor identifiers (recorder, approver, author, voider, withdrawer) denote people; the caller must make them pseudonymous references (stated obligation). Evidence is an identifier of a document kept elsewhere. No floating point is used.

## The exact primitive

`exact.Fraction` is a value with a numerator and a denominator above zero in lowest terms; no operation changes an operand or returns a number another fraction shares. Its zero value is not valid (`Valid`). `Whole(n)`; `Of(decimal)`, which refuses an invalid decimal; `Units(n, scale)`, n times ten to the minus scale, which refuses a missing n and a scale below zero; `Plus`, `Minus`, `Times`; `Over`, which refuses a divisor of zero; `Sign`; `Less`; `Digits`, the larger count of decimal digits of numerator and denominator; `Text`, `n` or `n/d`; and `Quanta(scale, mode)`, a new whole number of units of ten to the minus scale after one rounding under `toward_zero`, `away_from_zero`, `floor`, `ceiling`, `half_up` (a tie away from zero) or `half_even`, which refuses a scale below zero and a mode outside the list; `ValidMode(name)`. Modes are names, as the sibling modules store them, and not the primitive's `decimal.Mode` (provisional). No fraction is read back from its text; a fraction is kept only in memory.

## Observations

An observation is a rate as a source quoted it: source, an identifier; series, an identifier of the kind of rate of that source; base currency and quote currency, different; date; unit, one of 1, 10, 100, 1000, 10000, the number of units of the base the value is for; value, a number above zero, the amount of quote currency for that many units of base; origin, `feed`, `manual` or `file`; evidence, required for `manual` and `file` and empty for `feed`; recorder. Its key is source, series, base, quote and date. Under a key, revisions are numbered from 1 without a gap, and `RecordObservation` creates the key with revision 1. A revision is written once: an identical repeat writes nothing, anything else under the number is a conflict, a number beyond the next one is rejected, and the next one when the key holds as many revisions as the limit is a limit error. A correction is the next revision.

A revision of origin `feed` is `recorded`. One of origin `manual` or `file` is `awaiting_approval` until `RecordObservationApproval(workspace, key, revision, approver, reference)` makes it `approval_recorded`. `VoidObservation(workspace, key, revision, voider, evidence, reason)`, with a reason of `mistake`, `duplicate` or `source_withdrew`, marks a revision `void_awaiting_approval`, and `RecordVoidApproval(workspace, key, revision, approver, reference)` makes it `void`. An approver is never the actor who recorded what is approved. Approving a `feed` revision, a revision in another state than the one the approval is for, is rejected; an identical repeat of an approval or a void writes nothing in any later state; another approver, reference, voider, evidence or reason is a conflict.

Only the highest revision of a key decides what the key gives: `recorded` or `approval_recorded`, its value; `awaiting_approval` or `void_awaiting_approval`, nothing, with the reason `awaiting_approval`; `void`, no value, the date being passed over as N3 of the re-review resolution says. A revision below the highest is never used, so neither a void nor a correction awaiting approval uncovers an older value. The normalised rate of an observation is its value divided by its unit, exact.

## Policies

A policy is identified in a workspace and has, fixed for all its versions: a level, `customer`, `contract` or `msp`; a key, the customer identifier or the contract identifier, empty for `msp`; a purpose, `billing`, `display` or `accounting`; a source currency and a target currency, different. `RecordPolicyVersion` carries these attributes each time: version 1 creates the policy; attributes other than the stored ones are a conflict; a second policy identifier with the level, key, purpose and pair of another is a conflict. A version has a number from 1 without a gap, an effective date as N4 of the re-review resolution says, an author, and a method:

- `ended`, at the levels `customer` and `contract`: the policy states nothing from its effective date, so selection goes on as if it had no version;
- `default_allowed`, at the level `contract`: the contract leaves this purpose and pair to the MSP default from its effective date; it sets no spread of its own;
- `fixed`: a unit and a value as an observation has them, an evidence identifier, and an approval: a fixed version is `awaiting_approval` until `RecordPolicyApproval(workspace, policy, version, approver, reference)` by another than its author;
- `dated` or `monthly_average`, with a source and a series, and a construction: `direct`, from the observation with base the source currency and quote the target; `inverse`, from the observation with base the target and quote the source; `cross`, with a pivot currency other than both, from the observations of source in pivot and of target in pivot;
- for `dated`, the rule for a date without a value: `hold`, or `previous_recorded` with a maximum of elapsed days from 1 to 31;
- for `monthly_average`, the month, `same_month` or `previous_month` of the need's date, the averaging label `arithmetic_mean_of_recorded_days`, and the least number of days with a value, from 1 to 31, judged for each key used;
- for every method but `ended` and `default_allowed`: a spread, a number from 0 below 1; a fee rate, a number from 0 below 1; the scale of the sales quote, from 0 to 8 and within the profile, and its mode; a tolerance, a number above 0 and at most 0.01, the largest relative difference allowed between the rounded sales quote and the exact one; the quantum of the target amount (`1`, `0.1`, `0.01`, `0.001`, `0.0001`) and its mode; the order instruction `convert_after_source_charges`. All are required, zero being written.

`WithdrawPolicyVersion(workspace, policy, version, withdrawer, reason)`, with `mistake` or `replaced`, takes a version out. The version of a policy on a date is the one with the latest effective date not after it, withdrawn or not, found by a search over versions sorted by effective date. When that version is withdrawn, the policy is `withdrawn_in_force` on that date: selection holds with that reason, neither falling through to another level nor reviving an earlier version. A hole made by a withdrawal is closed only by a later version (cost to availability, stated).

## Needs and the selection of a policy

A need is what a run will convert under one rate: an identifier unique in its snapshot, a purpose, a source and a target currency, a customer and a contract, both required identifiers, and a date. `Prepare(workspace, snapshot, needs)` resolves each need once, in the order given, and stores the snapshot.

A need whose source is its target has an entry of kind `same_currency` and nothing else.

Otherwise a policy is selected, each step looking at the version of a policy on the need's date as defined above:

1. the policy of level `customer` for the need's customer, purpose and pair: a version that is `fixed`, `dated` or `monthly_average` is selected with reason `customer_exception`; `ended` or no version goes to step 2; `withdrawn_in_force` holds;
2. the policy of level `contract` for the need's contract, purpose and pair: a rate method is selected with reason `contract`; `default_allowed` goes to step 3 and is kept in the entry as the allowing policy and version; `ended`, no version or no policy holds with `contract_not_stated`; `withdrawn_in_force` holds;
3. the policy of level `msp` for the purpose and pair: a rate method is selected with reason `msp_default`; no version, or `ended`, holds with `no_default_policy`; `withdrawn_in_force` holds.

A customer exception is taken whatever the contract says (provisional). A pair has policies only in the direction stated; a policy for the reverse pair is not used.

## The rate of an entry

The calendar date of the registry under the need's offset is the registry's time plus the offset, as a date. A need dated after it holds with `date_not_reached`; a month that has not ended by it holds with `month_not_ended`. The registry's time is the highest reading of its clock: a reading ahead of the true time makes these two guards pass early until the true time catches up (the clock is trusted; stated).

The quoted rate is an exact fraction in its quoted orientation and unit:

- `fixed`: the version's value per its unit, when its approval is recorded; `awaiting_approval` holds;
- `direct` and `inverse`: the observation's value per its unit;
- `cross`: target per one unit of source, the normalised source-in-pivot rate divided by the normalised target-in-pivot rate, unit 1.

For `dated`, the observations are those of every key the construction needs on one date: the need's date when every key gives a value on it; otherwise, under `previous_recorded`, the nearest earlier date within the maximum of elapsed days on which every key gives a value; the search ends with `awaiting_approval` at the first date, the need's date included, on which a key gives that reason. Nothing found holds with `no_rate_recorded`, or with `legs_not_on_one_date` when each key has a value on some date in the window but no date has all. An entry that used an earlier date records `fallback`, the date used and the days elapsed.

For `monthly_average`, every day of the month on which a key gives `awaiting_approval` holds the entry with that reason, and a void day is left out; each key needs at least the least number of days with a value, else `not_enough_days`; the quoted rate is the arithmetic mean, in the quoted orientation, of the normalised values of each key over its own days, expressed per the largest unit used (N1), and for `cross` the quotient of the means of the two normalised legs. So an inverse average is one over the mean of the quotes, not the mean of the inverses. The entry records every date and revision used.

The sales quote applies the spread so that a customer receives less source for the same target and pays more target for the same source: for `direct`, `fixed` and `cross`, quote times one plus the spread; for `inverse`, quote divided by one plus the spread (provisional). It is rounded once to the scale of the sales quote under its mode, in the quoted orientation and per the quoted unit. It must be above zero, else `rate_rounds_to_zero`, and its relative difference from the exact sales quote must be at most the tolerance, else `rate_precision_lost`. The entry records the quoted rate as a fraction text, the unit, the spread, the exact and the rounded sales quote, and the relative difference as a fraction text.

The sales rate, target per one unit of source, is exact: the rounded sales quote divided by its unit for `direct`, `fixed` and `cross`, and the unit divided by the rounded sales quote for `inverse`. It is used without further rounding.

A whole number of a fraction beyond the configured digits is a limit error for the whole `Prepare`, and nothing is stored (cost to availability, stated).

## Snapshots

A snapshot has an identifier unique in the workspace, the registry's time when it was prepared, its entries in the order of the needs, the number of held entries, and two digests. Both are the SHA-256 of a print in which every field is written as its length in eight bytes, big-endian, then its bytes, numbers as decimal text without leading zeros, absent parts as empty fields, and every repeated part preceded by its count. The content digest prints the label `fx_snapshot_content_v1`, the workspace, and then for each entry: the need's identifier, purpose, source, target, customer, contract and date; the entry's kind (`same_currency`, `rate` or `held`); the reason; the selected level, policy and version; the allowing contract policy and version; the method, construction, pivot, month rule, averaging label, least days, offset, fallback rule and maximum days; the count of observations used and for each its source, series, base, quote, date, revision, unit, value, origin, evidence and approval reference; for a fixed rate its unit, value, evidence and approval reference; the date used and days elapsed; the quoted rate, spread, exact and rounded sales quote, the scale and mode of the quote, the tolerance, the relative difference, the sales rate, the fee rate, the quantum and mode of amounts, and the order instruction. The snapshot digest prints the label `fx_snapshot_v1`, the snapshot's identifier and the content digest. Profile and limits are not part of either; the time of preparation is not either.

So two snapshots of the same needs over the same facts have the same content digest and different snapshot digests, and a snapshot whose rates changed has another content digest. A snapshot is written once and never changes. `Prepare` with the identifier of a stored snapshot and identical needs in the same order returns it without resolving anything again, whatever was recorded since; with other needs it is a conflict.

## Conversion

`Convert(workspace, snapshot, lines)` reads the stored snapshot and nothing else, under the lock, and computes outside it. A line has an identifier unique in the call, the need it belongs to, an amount, which may be negative, a currency, and a state: `source`, or `already_in_target` for an amount the provider or the contract already expressed in the target currency. Every line is judged before any is computed, by this table:

| Need's entry | Line's state | Line's currency | Kind of result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `same_currency` | `source` or `already_in_target` | the need's currency | `not_converted_same_currency`, the amount as given |
| `rate` or `held` | `already_in_target` | the need's target | `not_converted_already_in_target`, the amount as given |
| `held` | `source` | the need's source | `held`, no amount, the entry's reason |
| `rate` | `source` | the need's source | `converted` |
| any | any | any other | the call is rejected |

For `converted`: the exact converted amount is the line's amount times the sales rate; the converted amount is that rounded once to the quantum under the amounts' mode; the amount at the quoted rate is the line's amount times the quoted rate per one unit of source, rounded the same way, for comparison; the fee is the exact converted amount times the fee rate, rounded the same way; the total is converted amount plus fee. Under `floor` and `ceiling` a credit does not mirror its charge (stated). The result carries the snapshot's identifier, both digests, the label `fx_v1` and the number of held lines. An amount returned unchanged is the text as given. An amount the profile cannot hold is a limit error for the call. One wrong line rejects the whole call without saying which (cost, stated). No operation takes a rate as a parameter, and no rate is read from anywhere but the snapshot.

## Reads

`Observation(workspace, key)` returns the revisions of a key with their states, approvals, voids, actors and times. `Policy(workspace, policy)` returns its fixed attributes and versions with approvals and withdrawals. `Snapshot(workspace, snapshot)` returns a copy of the snapshot. `Snapshots(workspace, after, limit)` lists identifiers in byte order after an identifier, with both digests. Copies of anything long are made outside the lock.

## Repeats, error kinds, tenant boundary, time and limits

A request is judged in this order: its sizes, a limit error (the number of needs or lines, before any is read); its form by what the request alone shows, invalid (a revision or version number of zero or beyond its bound, a scale beyond the profile, an evidence present for `feed` or absent for `manual` and `file`, a field a method does not have); what it names looked up, not found, also for another workspace's (a key or policy that does not exist and is not created by this call, a need of a line that the snapshot does not have); a repeat when identical and a conflict otherwise; what stored state or a rule refuses, rejected (a number beyond the next, an effective date not later than every earlier one, an approver who recorded what is approved, an approval of a state it is not for); no room, a limit error. Errors are fixed values and repeat no string of the request.

Every operation takes the workspace and reaches only that workspace's observations, policies and snapshots; a source is a name inside a workspace. The registry authorizes nothing and must not be exposed directly (exposure blocker). It is built with a valid decimal profile, limits and a clock; its time is the highest reading of the clock, in UTC. Limits, each required, above zero and with an upper bound that fits a 32-bit `int`: keys of observations per workspace (1,000,000), revisions per key (1,000), policies per workspace (100,000), versions per policy (10,000), needs per snapshot (10,000), snapshots per workspace (100,000), lines per conversion (100,000), digits of a whole number (10,000), page size (1,000). The highest revision of a key is its last, read in constant time, and a version on a date is found by a binary search; so a `Prepare` costs at most needs times three policies' searches and two keys times 31 days, which runs under the lock, accepted. Memory is not bounded by these limits, which bound counts and not the product of snapshots and their entries; the number of workspaces is the caller's bound.

## What is not decided here

Provisional defaults, each to be confirmed or changed by the owner: the three levels, that an exception needs nothing from the contract and that `ended` falls through; that a contract without a policy holds; `previous_recorded` in place of a business-day calendar, with every leg on one date; that a correction or a void awaiting approval holds; the arithmetic mean of quoted values over recorded days, a cross as the quotient of means; a fixed calendar offset per version; the direction of the spread for an inverse; rounding the sales quote once in its quoted orientation with a tolerance, and each amount once; the fee from the exact converted amount; modes as names. Not conservative, stated: an observation's value and origin are a caller's statement; a feed observation needs no approval; nothing ties an actor to a person; a policy version that is not fixed needs no approval here; the registry's clock is trusted. Costs to availability: an entry is held rather than estimated; one correction or void awaiting approval holds every need of its key; a withdrawal holds its dates until a later version; a snapshot is never amended, a run needing a new one; one need beyond the digits fails a whole `Prepare`; one wrong line fails a whole conversion. Assumptions: currencies are three-letter codes compared as written; dates are calendar dates given by the caller in the policy's calendar.

# Design review resolution

The independent design review of 2026-11-13 returned FAIL without need for redesign, with findings F1 to F18. The sections above are the corrected design.

| --- | --- |

# Design re-review resolution

The independent re-review of 2026-11-13 returned FAIL with one blocker (N1) that needs no redesign, and findings N1 to N13. The rules below are part of the design and prevail over the sections above where they differ.

- N1, units in an average: the monthly average is the arithmetic mean of the normalised values (value divided by unit) of each key in its quoted orientation, expressed per the largest unit among the observations it used, which is the unit of its quoted rate. So 905.25 per 100 on one day and 9.0525 per 1 on another average to 905.25 per 100.
- N2, today: a need dated on the registry's local date uses that date's value when every key gives one, and otherwise holds with `date_not_reached`; an earlier date is used only for a need dated before the local date. A month counts as ended at the local midnight after its last day; a day of it without any revision is taken as a day without a rate. The registry cannot tell a holiday from a feed not yet loaded; the least number of days is the only guard of an average (residual risk, stated).
- N3, an approved void: a `void` revision gives no value on its date and is passed over like a date without one: the dated search goes on to an earlier date within the bound, and the average leaves the day out. A void awaiting approval holds. The entry records the number of approved voids passed over, and the content digest prints it.
- N4, the hole after a withdrawal: a new version's effective date must be later than that of every earlier version that is not withdrawn and not earlier than that of any withdrawn one. Versions are ordered by effective date and then by number, and the version of a policy on a date is the last in that order whose effective date is not after it. So a later version with the effective date of a withdrawn one closes its hole from that date.
- N5, the comparison amount: the normalised quoted rate is target per one unit of source: value divided by unit for `direct` and `fixed` in the direct orientation, unit divided by value for `inverse` and `fixed` in the inverse orientation, and for `cross` the quotient of the normalised legs. The amount at the quoted rate is the line's amount times it, rounded like the converted amount.
- N6, a total order of hold reasons, the first met deciding: `date_not_reached` or `month_not_ended`; the selection's reasons in the order of the steps; for a fixed version, `awaiting_approval`; for observations, dates in the order searched (from the need's date backwards for `dated`, from the first day of the month for `monthly_average`) and within a date the keys in the order of the construction (the source leg before the target leg), the first `awaiting_approval` or `void_awaiting_approval` met holding with `awaiting_approval`; then `no_rate_recorded` or `legs_not_on_one_date`; `not_enough_days`; `rate_rounds_to_zero`; `rate_precision_lost`. The reason `voided` is no longer used. An approval, a void or a withdrawal of a revision or version that does not exist is not found.
- N7, the tolerance: the relative difference is the distance between the rounded and the exact sales quote divided by the smaller of the two, which bounds the relative error of the quote and of the sales rate alike.
- N8, the calendar: the offset is a field of the need, from −720 to +840 minutes, the calendar in which the caller states the need's date; versions do not carry it. The entry records it and the content digest prints it.
- N9, fixed rates: a fixed version has an orientation, `direct` or `inverse`, in its construction field; an inverse fixed rate is a value of source per unit of target and is used like an inverse observation.
- N10: the description no longer claims that an amount is never converted twice.
- N11: `ended` exists at the levels `customer` and `contract` only, `default_allowed` at `contract` only, and a version with another method for its level is invalid. A fixed version may be withdrawn awaiting approval; approving a withdrawn version is rejected; an identical repeat of a withdrawal writes nothing and another withdrawer or reason is a conflict.
- N12: every number text is stored and printed as the decimal primitive's canonical print, without trailing zeros, so `1350.00` and `1350` are the same value, and a repeat is compared on canonical texts.
- N13: the fee of a negative line is negative, the fee being refunded with the charge (provisional). A cross is rounded in its converting orientation and is refused, not degraded, when it loses more than the tolerance. The property that both legs share a date applies to `dated`. The last row of the conversion table means any combination not listed above. A method of the exact primitive called on a zero-value fraction panics; callers keep only fractions it returned.
- Tests added to Verification: mixed units within a key; a need dated today with and without a value, and a run just after a month ended; an approved void of each reason in `dated` and in `monthly_average`; a later version with a withdrawn version's effective date; the inverse comparison amount; ties between hold reasons; the tolerance at its boundary and the inverse case of N7; offsets in the need; `rate_rounds_to_zero` and a cross refused for precision; a method not allowed for its level; approval and withdrawal of a fixed version in each state; equal content digests across preparation times and canonical number texts; the fee of a negative line.
