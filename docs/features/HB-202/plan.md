# Scope and ownership

# Observed baseline

Facts read on 2026-11-13:

- Requirement HB-14: the amount to allocate equals what customers or organisations are allocated, what the MSP or operations bear and an explicit residual, conserved per currency. Showback, a simulation of an allocation and a confirmed chargeback are different things. Proposed direction: AllocationPoolVersion and AllocationRun; a fixed priority of direct attribution, tag, ratio, usage, reservation and common cost; the assignment of a rounding remainder decided by a contractual rule that includes the order of stable identifiers. Scale: compute the denominator of the whole shared pool first, write the results of chunks to staging, and publish or confirm no result per customer before the conservation of all chunks is checked.
- Acceptance of HB-14: the same allocation whatever the order of input, the number of workers and the size of chunks; a zero denominator is held, or settled by an approved alternative; a residual below 100% is tracked; no customer is issued ahead of the others. Linked source cases COM04 (period cost = allocated use + unused cost, a residual tracked with its reason), COM06 (cost to allocate = customers or organisations + MSP + explicitly unattributed), GH03 (shares and residual of a shared source are conserved per company and an overlapping group does not raise the charge) and GH06 are NOT_RUN.
- Open decisions of HB-14, not made: the denominator, the assignment of a residual, the boundaries of a pool, what the order of steps means.
- Existing code: `modules/allocation` has `LargestRemainderV1`, which distributes a quantized total over weighted targets, breaks ties by target identifier in bytewise order, returns shares in the order of identifiers and verifies conservation. `modules/costpool` (HB-198) allocates an on-premises pool with one basis at the record of an approval and has the patterns reused here: quanta as a closed list, a total that must be a whole multiple of the quantum so that nothing is left to rounding, limit errors for bounds, a self-check error, a stored invariant instead of a conflict judged at reading.

# Decision

One Go package, `modules/sharedpool`, standard library, the decimal primitive and `modules/allocation` only, with an in-memory registry. It has no entry point wired in this increment: a caller opens a version of a pool, starts a run, stages the weights of the run's targets in chunks, seals the run and publishes it.

What this can and cannot show: that the shares of a sealed run add up to the pool's amount exactly, each target having one share; that they are the same whatever the order in which chunks and weights arrived and however the targets were cut into chunks, because nothing is computed before every declared chunk is there and the allocation is one call over the targets in the order of their identifiers; that a run whose denominator is zero or has a weight that is not known is held and has no share; that what lies below a stated capacity is a share of its own with a bearer; that no share of a run can be read before the run is published; and that at any one reading at most one published chargeback run of a pool covers a moment of time, which is a stored invariant. It cannot know that a weight, a capacity or an amount is true, that the targets staged are all there are, that two pools do not hold the same cost, or that a published run was already used. Staging by several callers at once is what "the number of workers" can mean inside this package; processes and their failure are outside it.

Names. A run has a purpose, `showback`, `simulation` or `chargeback`; the purpose changes nothing in the arithmetic and only decides which runs exclude each other. A published chargeback run is what the requirement calls confirmed; the state is called `published`, since the record of an approver and a reference is a caller's statement and nothing here checks who approved. `sealed` says that the shares were computed and conserve the amount, not that they are right.

Not delivered, although the requirement names it: a fixed priority of direct attribution, tag, ratio, usage, reservation and common cost. A pool version has one basis; its kind is a label of a closed list that no rule uses, and allocation in several ordered steps is deferred. Also not delivered: group rules and what GH06 asks; re-approval after a retroactive change; staging that survives a process. The design part of HB-14 stays open with the owner. W-14 asks for independent verification by a DBA and by QA; this ticket has one independent reviewer of design and change, and the rest remains.

Alternatives considered. Allocating each chunk as it arrives against a denominator stated in advance: not taken, a stated denominator that the chunks then do not add up to gives shares that do not conserve, and a per-chunk rounding makes the result depend on the cut. Showing the shares of a sealed run before publication: not taken, it is the early issue the acceptance forbids. A rounding remainder borne by nobody: not taken, an amount must be a whole multiple of the quantum, as in HB-198.

Every caller-supplied string is an identifier under the project's identifier rule (letters, digits, `. _ : -`, at most 128 bytes), of which the package has its own copy that a test compares with the allocation's, a three-letter uppercase currency code, the text of an amount, or a value of a closed list. There is no free text. A target is an identifier resolved nowhere; the caller must make it a pseudonymous reference (stated obligation). No floating point is used. All times are kept and returned in UTC; periods are half-open.

What a workspace is, the MSP's scope or one customer's, is not decided here. The shares of all targets of a published run are readable by whoever may call the registry with its workspace; it must not be exposed to a customer-facing read (exposure blocker).

## Forms

An amount, a weight and a capacity are texts in the form the decimal primitive parses with the registry's profile, never starting with a minus sign; a pool's amount has at most four digits after the point. A quantum is one of `1`, `0.1`, `0.01`, `0.001`, `0.0001`, limited by the profile's input scale. Texts are kept as written, returned as written, and compared as texts in a repeat. Sums and shares are returned as the primitive prints them. Absent is the empty text.

## Pool versions

`OpenPool(spec)` creates a version of a pool. A header has: workspace, pool and version number (1 to 999,999), its identity; a currency; a period of whole seconds that runs forward and is not longer than the configured maximum; the amount; the quantum; a basis kind (`direct`, `tag`, `ratio`, `usage`, `reservation`, `common`), a label; and how the denominator is formed, one of two ways. With a stated capacity, greater than zero, the denominator is that capacity and a residual bearer, `msp` or `unattributed`, is required: what the weights leave of the capacity is a residual borne by it. With no capacity (the empty text) the denominator is the sum of the weights, the residual bearer must be empty, and there is no residual; this is normalisation, chosen openly. The header is immutable. Malformed, among the rest: an amount that is not a whole multiple of the quantum, which the request alone shows.

## Runs and chunks

`StartRun(workspace, pool, version, run)` starts a run with an identifier unique in the pool version, a purpose and the number of chunks it will have, at least 1 and at most the limit. Its state is `staging`.

`StageChunk(workspace, pool, version, run, number, weights)` stages one chunk under its number, from 1 to the declared number. A weight has a target identifier, a bearer class (`organization`, `msp`, `unattributed`) and a weight that may be not known (the empty text). A target identifier must not start with a colon, which is reserved. Malformed: an empty chunk, a target twice in a chunk, a number below 1. A chunk is written once under its number: an identical repeat writes nothing and succeeds in every state, the order of its weights not being part of the comparison; a differing one is a conflict. A new chunk is rejected unless the run is staging, the number is within the declared number, and none of its targets is in another chunk of the run. So a target has one weight in a run.

`Seal(workspace, pool, version, run)` is rejected unless the run is staging and every declared chunk is staged. It first forms the denominator over all chunks and judges, in this order: a weight that is not known holds the run with `weight_not_known`; a denominator of zero, which only a pool without a stated capacity can have, holds it with `zero_denominator`; weights that add up to more than a stated capacity hold it with `over_capacity`. A held run has no share. Otherwise the amount is allocated by `LargestRemainderV1` in one call over all targets, with the pool's quantum, and with one more target when a stated capacity is not used up: the residual, under the reserved identifier `:residual`, with the unused capacity as its weight and the pool's residual bearer. The residual takes part in the distribution of units left over like any target, in the bytewise order of identifiers. The run becomes `sealed`, and stores the shares, the sum per bearer class, and the residual apart with the reason `below_capacity`. After the allocation the registry checks on its own that the shares add up to the amount and that the class sums do. A bound of the profile or of the allocation is a limit error and the run stays staging; a failed self-check or any other error of the allocation is a sixth fixed error; in both cases nothing is written.

`Settle(workspace, pool, version, run, alternative)` is the approved alternative for a zero denominator: an approver, a reference and a bearer, `msp` or `unattributed`. It is rejected unless the run is held with `zero_denominator`. The whole amount becomes one share, the residual, with that bearer and the reason `approved_alternative`, and the run becomes `sealed`. A run held for another reason has no alternative here: it is withdrawn and another run is started.

`Publish(workspace, pool, version, run, approval)` takes an approver and a reference and is rejected unless the run is sealed. A chargeback run is also rejected while another chargeback run of the same pool, of any version, is published and its version's period overlaps this one's. So of two chargeback runs of one period one is published; showback and simulation runs are published freely and exclude nothing. The run becomes `published`.

`Withdraw(workspace, pool, version, run, reason)` takes any run that is not withdrawn out, with a reason of a closed list (`entered_in_error`, `superseded`, `operator`). It is the exit of a held run, of a sealed run that should not be published, and of a published chargeback run that another is to replace.

## Reading

`Run` returns the run's identifier, purpose, state, the declared and staged numbers of chunks, the number of targets, the reason of a hold, the alternative, the approval, the reason of a withdrawal, times, and, once sealed, the amount, the denominator, the sum per bearer class and the residual with its reason. It returns no share. `Shares(…, after, limit)` returns the shares of a run in the order of target identifiers, each with bearer, weight as written, amount and whether it is the residual, and is rejected unless the run is published; a published run that was withdrawn stays readable. `Chunk(…, number, after, limit)` returns the staged weights of a chunk in any state, since they are the caller's own input. `Runs` returns the runs of a pool version in the order of identifiers. `Chargeback(workspace, pool, start, end)` reads the published chargeback run of the pool whose version has exactly that period, with its version and a sequence number of the pool that every changing write raises by one, also when nothing is published; for a pool that does not exist it is not found with the number zero.

## Repeats, error kinds, tenant boundary, time and limits

Every operation first tests for an identical repeat, which writes nothing, succeeds in every state reached since and returns that state. A second `Settle` or `Publish` with other content and a second `Withdraw` with another reason are conflicts. `Seal` on a run that is sealed, held, published or withdrawn after a seal is a repeat and returns the state.

A request is judged in this order: malformed by what the request alone shows, which is invalid; what it names looked up, an unknown pool, version or run, or one of another workspace, being not found; a limit on what the request itself carries, the weights of one chunk, a limit error; an identifier that exists, a repeat when identical and a conflict otherwise; what a stored state or rule refuses, rejected; no room, a limit error. Errors are fixed values and repeat no string of the request.

Every operation takes the workspace and reaches only that workspace's pools. The registry authorizes nothing and must not be exposed directly. It is built with limits, a valid decimal profile and a clock, all required; its time is the highest reading of the clock. Limits, each greater than zero: pools per workspace, versions per pool, runs per version, chunks per run, weights per chunk, targets per run (also the allocation's, the residual included), the page size, the longest period, and the digits of an intermediate in the allocation. Withdrawn runs count towards the limit for good. One lock guards the registry; `Seal` sorts and allocates the targets of one run while it is held, bounded by the targets per run.

## What is not decided here

Provisional defaults, each to be confirmed or changed by the owner: a residual is borne by the MSP or left unattributed, never spread over organisations unless the pool is opened as normalising; a zero denominator is held and its alternative gives the whole amount to one bearer; an amount must be a whole multiple of the quantum; purposes do not differ in arithmetic; one chargeback per pool and overlapping period. Not conservative, stated: a pool opened as normalising silently spreads unused capacity over its targets; nothing relates two pools; a weight of zero is a stated zero and gives a share of zero. Costs to availability: one weight that is not known holds the whole run; a missing chunk keeps every target waiting; a published chargeback must be withdrawn before another of an overlapping period is published. Assumptions: amounts and weights are never negative; one currency per pool version; one weight per target and run.

Shares are checked against properties and not a second allocation: they add up to the amount; each is a multiple of the quantum and differs from its exact proportion of the amount by less than one quantum; a larger weight never has a smaller share; a weight of zero has a share of zero; bearer and weight of each share are what was staged. - The package's tests run many times in a row before any mutation run; a mutation check by a script in Python with results appended to files that are never overwritten, kept outside the repository.

# Design review resolution

The independent design review of 2026-11-13 (read-only, nothing executed) returned PASS with findings, no blocker and no redesign: no HIGH, 12 MEDIUM, 10 LOW. It found no sequence of operations after which a sealed or published run has shares that do not add up to the amount and no path to two published chargeback runs of one pool with overlapping periods, and it confirmed by reading the allocation that the shares of an accepted set of weights do not depend on order. It asked for text fixes before implementation. The rules below settle the findings and are part of the decision; where they differ from a sentence above, they govern. The change review reads them against the code.

- F1, the target limit. A run admits as many targets as the limit; the allocation is called with one more, so a residual never exceeds it.
- F2, the sequence of `Seal` and its errors. After the rejections: a weight that is not known holds the run, before any sum; the weights are added, a bound of the profile being a limit error; then `zero_denominator` or `over_capacity`; then the allocation, a bound being a limit error; then the self-check. `decimal.ErrResultBound`, `decimal.ErrIntermediateBound` and `allocation.ErrIntermediateBound` are the limit error; every other error of the allocation or the primitive is the self-check error; no foreign error value is returned. The allocation is called with the mode `decimal.HalfEven`, which has no effect on an exact multiple. A run whose seal ends in a limit error stays staging and can never be sealed, since nothing staged can change: its only exit is `Withdraw`.
- F3, `Settle`. Every staged target has a share of zero and the residual has the whole amount. So in every sealed run each staged target has exactly one share.
- F4, sums. Two identities hold for a sealed run and are checked by the registry: the amount is the sum of the three class sums, the residual being inside the sum of its bearer's class (COM06 as arithmetic); and the amount is the sum of the shares that are not the residual plus the residual (COM04 as arithmetic only: unused cost that a caller stages as a target of its own is not known to be unused). The residual is an of-which figure and must not be added to the class sums. It is absent when the run has no residual target, which is not a residual of zero. The residual's weight is returned as the primitive prints it.
- F5, a provisional default: the residual's identifier sorts after targets that start with a digit, a hyphen or a point and before those that start with a letter or an underscore, so on equal remainders a unit left over goes to it before any target named with a letter. Who bears a unit of rounding thus depends on how targets are named, until the owner decides a rule.
- F6, what does not depend on order. The claim is for runs whose every chunk was accepted as first submitted. When two chunks carry the same target, or one number is submitted twice with other content, the first to arrive is kept and the other refused, so with an inconsistent source the result depends on arrival (stated cost; the refusal makes it visible).
- F7, what is readable when. Before publication `Run` returns no sum per class and no residual: it returns state, counts, the amount, the denominator once formed and what stands against a seal. Class sums and the residual are returned with the shares, once published. So nothing computed for a target or a class is readable before publication.
- F8, review before publication. A run cannot be inspected and then published as chargeback. The route is a `simulation` run of the same weights, published and read, which by the order independence has the same shares and excludes nothing; a chargeback run is then published without having been read (stated cost). The alternative of showing sealed shares was not taken for this reason of scope, not because the acceptance forbids an operator's read of a checked run.
- F9, pages of shares return the run's state, purpose and the pool's sequence number. A billing reader goes through `Chargeback` and compares the number before and after reading the pages.
- F10, the relation to `modules/costpool`. Its allocation at the record of an approval is the case of one chunk, a stated capacity and all weights known. The two differ on unused capacity: there the caller must name it as a target with an idle mark and weights must equal the capacity; here the registry makes the residual itself. This is a known divergence for the owner. Nothing here reads a cost pool, and whether a cost pool's total becomes a pool amount here is not decided. Extracting what the two share, the identifier rule, the quanta and the self-check, is deferred; `modules/costpool` is not touched.
- F11. A pool version has no basis kind: the field is removed, and how weights were derived is not recorded.
- F12, names. The residual's second reason is `recorded_alternative`. A published chargeback run stands in the place of what the requirement calls confirmed; that an authorised approver confirmed is not met by this package. The reasons of a withdrawal are `entered_in_error` and `operator`. `Publish` requires an approver and a reference for a chargeback run and requires both empty for a showback or simulation run, which is judged against the stored purpose and so rejected when broken. `sealed` also covers a settled run, for which nothing was allocated.
- F13, reads added: `Pool` returns a version's header and `Versions` the versions of a pool in the order of numbers. `Run` of a staging or held run returns the number of weights not known and, for a held run, the sum of the weights beside the capacity. The number of targets does not include the residual.
- F14, how the tests reach shares: by publishing, a simulation run being publishable in every case, and for chargeback runs that an overlap keeps from publication through the package's own state. The properties cover the residual as a target whose weight is the unused capacity; the order of ties is pinned by literal tests only; the check against a second registry with shuffled chunks finds dependence on order and cut and no defect common to both. The self-check error is produced by no test.
- F15, repeats. The order of judging is the one stated: malformed, lookup, the size of a chunk, then repeat or conflict. A `Seal` that ends in a hold is a successful write that returns `held`. A run that a seal has judged, whatever its state now, answers `Seal` with its state. `StartRun` compares purpose and declared number.
- F16, classification. Malformed: a period longer than the maximum, for `OpenPool` and `Chargeback`; a declared number of chunks below 1; a page limit below 1 or above the page size; a capacity without a bearer and the reverse. A limit error on what the request carries: a declared number of chunks above the limit, and the weights of one chunk above theirs, counted before the weights are read.
- F17, a provisional default: a pool is one currency per period, since chargeback runs of a pool exclude each other whatever their versions' currencies; another currency needs another pool identifier.
- F19, the lock and limits. Published chargeback runs are kept per pool, so `Publish` and `Chargeback` look at those only. Workspaces are not limited in number. One lock guards the registry, so a large seal of one workspace delays the others (stated). An exhausted version needs another version and an exhausted pool another pool identifier.
- F20, pages and forms. `Runs` returns what comes after a given identifier, `Chunk` the weights after a given target, `Shares` the shares after a given target, for which `:residual` is accepted as a position; all up to a limit, in the order of identifiers, the empty identifier asking for the first page. A run's times are when it was started and last changed. `Chargeback` returns the version number, the run identifier and the pool's sequence number, and when nothing is published only the number with not found. The source scan allows of the primitive: `Parse`, `Add`, `Sub`, `Cmp`, the methods `Scale`, `Sign`, `String` and `Valid`, the types `Decimal` and `Profile`, the mode `HalfEven` and the two bound errors.
- F21. With a stated capacity and all weights zero the whole amount goes to the residual without a hold (not conservative, stated). The self-check also requires that the allocation reports the expected denominator and no rounding component.
- F22. Approver identifiers denote people; the caller must make them pseudonymous references (stated obligation).

It found no sequence of operations that breaks conservation, gives a target two shares or none, publishes two overlapping chargeback runs of a pool, or lets a share, a class sum or a residual amount be read early, and it held the removal of a sort before the allocation correct by argument. All findings were resolved or stated; the corrections were not reviewed again by a separate reviewer.
